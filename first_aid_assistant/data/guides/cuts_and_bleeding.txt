TITLE: Cuts, Wounds and Bleeding
SOURCE: Compiled from public guidance of the NHS, Mayo Clinic, American Red Cross and St John Ambulance
---
Most small cuts and scrapes can be treated at home. The main aims are to stop the bleeding, keep the wound clean and prevent infection.

Steps for a minor cut:
Wash your hands and wear gloves if available.
Apply firm direct pressure with a clean cloth or gauze for ten minutes. Raise the injured arm or leg above the level of the heart.
When bleeding has stopped, rinse the wound under clean running water, pat dry and cover with a sterile dressing.
Check that tetanus vaccination is up to date, especially for dirty wounds.

Severe bleeding:
Call emergency services if blood is spurting, soaking through bandages or does not stop after ten minutes of firm pressure.
Keep pressing on the wound. If blood soaks through, add more material on top rather than removing the first layer.
Lay the person down, keep them warm and watch for signs of shock such as pale cold skin, fast pulse and confusion.
Never pull out an object that is embedded in a wound. Pad around it and bandage to hold it still.

See a doctor for deep or gaping wounds that may need stitches, animal or human bites, very dirty wounds, or signs of infection such as pus, swelling, spreading redness or fever.
