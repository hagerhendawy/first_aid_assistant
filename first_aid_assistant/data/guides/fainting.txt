TITLE: Fainting (Syncope)
SOURCE: Compiled from public guidance of the NHS, Mayo Clinic, American Red Cross and British Red Cross
---
Fainting is a short loss of consciousness caused by a temporary reduction of blood flow to the brain. Common triggers are standing up quickly, heat, dehydration, emotional stress, pain or not eating.

If a person feels faint, help them sit or lie down before they collapse.

If a person has fainted:
Lay them flat on their back and raise their legs about 30 centimetres to help blood return to the brain.
Loosen tight clothing and make sure they have fresh air.
Check that they are breathing normally.
Do not give food or drink until they are fully alert. When they wake, keep them lying for about ten minutes then help them sit up slowly.

Call emergency services if the person does not wake up within one minute, is not breathing, has chest pain, has hit their head, has a seizure, is pregnant or diabetic with no quick recovery, or has difficulty breathing. If unconscious but breathing, place them in the recovery position. If not breathing, begin CPR.

See a doctor if it is the first time they have fainted, it happens repeatedly or there was no obvious cause. To prevent fainting drink enough water, eat regular meals and stand up slowly.
