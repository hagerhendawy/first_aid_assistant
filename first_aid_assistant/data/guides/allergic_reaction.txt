TITLE: Allergic Reactions and Anaphylaxis
SOURCE: Compiled from public guidance of the NHS, Mayo Clinic and Allergy UK
---
Allergic reactions to foods, insect stings or medicines range from mild to life-threatening.

Mild reactions cause itching, hives, a rash or local swelling. Move away from the trigger, remove a bee stinger by scraping it sideways, wash the area, apply a cold pack and take an antihistamine as directed. Watch the person for an hour.

Anaphylaxis is a severe reaction. Warning signs are difficulty breathing or wheezing, swelling of the lips, tongue, face or throat, faintness, and a rapid pulse. Call emergency services immediately.
If an adrenaline auto-injector is available, use it at once in the outer thigh and note the time. A second dose can be given after five minutes if there is no improvement and emergency help has not arrived.
Lay the person flat with legs raised, or sitting up if breathing is difficult. Do not let them stand or walk. Be ready to start CPR.
