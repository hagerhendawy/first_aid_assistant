TITLE: Nosebleed
SOURCE: Compiled from public guidance of the NHS and Mayo Clinic
---
Nosebleeds are common and are usually not serious. Most come from small blood vessels at the front of the nose.

What to do:
Sit upright and lean slightly forward. Do not tilt the head back because blood can run down the throat.
Pinch the soft part of the nose just below the bony bridge for ten to fifteen minutes without releasing.
Breathe through the mouth and spit out any blood in the mouth. A cold pack on the bridge of the nose may help.
Do not blow the nose or pick it for several hours afterwards.

Seek medical help if bleeding continues for more than twenty minutes, follows a blow to the head or face, is very heavy, or happens to someone taking blood thinning medicines.
