TITLE: Burns and Scalds
SOURCE: Compiled from public guidance of the British Red Cross, NHS, Mayo Clinic and American Red Cross
---
Burns happen when skin is damaged by heat, hot liquids, chemicals, electricity or radiation. A scald is a burn from hot liquid or steam.

What to do for a minor burn:
Move the person away from the source of heat and remove rings, watches or tight clothing near the burn, unless stuck to the skin.
Cool the burn under cool running water for at least 10 and ideally 20 minutes. Do not use ice or iced water because it can damage the skin further.
Cover the burn loosely with clean cling film or a clean non-fluffy dressing to protect it from infection.
Do not apply butter, toothpaste, oil or creams. Do not burst blisters.
Simple pain relief such as paracetamol or ibuprofen can be taken as directed on the label.

When a burn is an emergency:
Large blisters, a burn bigger than the person's hand, or white, waxy or charred skin indicate a deep burn and need emergency care.
Burns on the face, hands, feet, joints or genitals need urgent medical attention.
Chemical and electrical burns are always serious. Switch off electrical power before touching the person. Rinse chemical burns with running water for at least 20 minutes.
All burns in babies and young children should be checked by a doctor.
