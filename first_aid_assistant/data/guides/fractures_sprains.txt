TITLE: Broken Bones and Sprains
SOURCE: Compiled from public guidance of the NHS, Mayo Clinic and American Red Cross
---
A fracture is a break in a bone. A sprain is a stretched or torn ligament. They can look similar, so any significant injury should be treated as a possible fracture until an X-ray proves otherwise.

Signs of a fracture include severe pain, swelling, bruising, a deformed limb, a snap or crack heard at the time of injury and being unable to move or put weight on the limb.

What to do:
Keep the injured part still. Support it in the position found with padding or a sling and do not try to straighten it.
Apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth for about twenty minutes to reduce swelling.
Do not give food or drink in case surgery is needed.
Go to the clinic or hospital for an X-ray.

Call emergency services if a bone is visible through the skin, the limb is badly deformed, there is heavy bleeding, or the fingers or toes are numb, pale or cold. Cover an open wound with a clean dressing and do not push the bone back.
