TITLE: Choking (Adults, Children and Babies)
SOURCE: Compiled from public guidance of the American Heart Association, British Red Cross, NHS and Mayo Clinic
---
Choking happens when an object blocks the airway. A person who can cough, speak or breathe has a mild blockage. A person who cannot breathe, speak or cough, or who goes blue, has a severe blockage and needs immediate help.

Mild choking:
Encourage the person to keep coughing. Do not slap their back while they are coughing effectively and do not put fingers into their mouth.

Severe choking in an adult or child over one year:
Ask someone to call emergency services.
Lean the person forward and give up to five sharp back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
If this fails, give up to five abdominal thrusts. Stand behind the person, place a fist above the navel, grasp it with the other hand and pull sharply inward and upward.
Alternate five back blows and five abdominal thrusts until the object is cleared or the person becomes unconscious.
For pregnant women or very large people, give chest thrusts instead of abdominal thrusts.

Baby under one year:
Lay the baby face-down along your forearm with the head lower than the body. Give up to five back blows, then turn the baby over and give five chest thrusts with two fingers. Never give abdominal thrusts to a baby.

If the person becomes unconscious, lower them to the floor and start CPR. Anyone who has needed abdominal thrusts should be checked by a doctor afterwards.
