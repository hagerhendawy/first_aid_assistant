# RUN 2 - Typed question: 'My friend has a burn with large blisters, what should I do?'
##############################################################################
==============================================================================
[!] FOR AWARENESS ONLY - this is NOT a substitute for professional medical care.
    In any serious case CONTACT EMERGENCY SERVICES IMMEDIATELY (Egypt ambulance: 123 | International: 112).
==============================================================================
Understood facts : Burn or scald, Large blisters

DOES THIS CASE NEED A HOSPITAL?
  YES - this may need EMERGENCY / hospital care. Call emergency services now.

WHAT SHOULD I DO?
  1. Call emergency services now (Egypt ambulance 123, international 112) or
     ask someone nearby to call.
  2. Keep cooling the burn while waiting; do not burst the blisters.
  3. Move away from the heat source. Remove rings, watches and tight clothing
     near the burn unless stuck to the skin.
  4. Cool the burn under cool (not ice-cold) running water for 20 minutes
     (minimum 10).
  5. Cover loosely with clean cling film or a non-fluffy sterile dressing.
  6. Do NOT use ice, butter, toothpaste or creams. Do NOT burst blisters.
  7. Use simple over-the-counter pain relief according to the label if needed.

WHY (rules fired):
  [B1] IF burn THEN SELF_CARE - A minor burn can be treated with cooling and a
     clean dressing.
  [B2] IF burn AND large_blisters THEN EMERGENCY - Large blisters suggest a
     deeper (second-degree) burn.

# RUN 3 - Symptom selection: choking + cannot breathe/speak + baby
##############################################################################
==============================================================================

==============================================================================
Understood facts : Cannot breathe, speak or cough, Choking, Patient is a baby (under 1 year), Patient is a young child

DOES THIS CASE NEED A HOSPITAL?
  YES - this may need EMERGENCY / hospital care. Call emergency services now.

WHAT SHOULD I DO?
  1. Call emergency services now (Egypt ambulance 123, international 112) or
     ask someone nearby to call.
  2. BABY under 1 year: sit down and lay the baby face-down along your
     forearm, head lower than the body, supporting the jaw.
  3. Give up to 5 back blows between the shoulder blades. Turn the baby over
     and give 5 chest thrusts with two fingers on the centre of the chest.
  4. Do NOT give abdominal thrusts to a baby. Repeat; start infant CPR if the
     baby becomes unresponsive.
  5. Even if the object comes out, have the baby checked by a doctor.

WHY (rules fired):
  [D1] IF infant THEN derive young_child - A baby is also a young child.
  [K2] IF choking AND cannot_breathe_speak THEN EMERGENCY - A blocked airway
     is life-threatening.
  [K4] IF choking AND cannot_breathe_speak AND infant THEN EMERGENCY - Infants
     need a different technique (replaces the adult steps of K2).

# RUN 4 - Typed question: 'She fainted but woke up after a few seconds, does she need a hospital?'
##############################################################################
==============================================================================
[!] FOR AWARENESS ONLY - this is NOT a substitute for professional medical care.
    In any serious case CONTACT EMERGENCY SERVICES IMMEDIATELY (Egypt ambulance: 123 | International: 112).
==============================================================================
Understood facts : Fainting / collapse, Woke up within a minute

DOES THIS CASE NEED A HOSPITAL?
  Probably manageable with first aid on the spot, but get medical help if it
  gets worse.

WHAT SHOULD I DO?
  1. Lay the person flat on their back and raise their legs about 30 cm.
     Loosen tight clothing and give fresh air.
  2. Check breathing. Do not give food or drink until fully alert.
  3. Let them stay lying for about 10 minutes, then sit up slowly before
     standing.
  4. If it was the first time or had no clear cause (heat, standing up
     quickly, dehydration), see a doctor.
  5. Keep the person hydrated and watch them for the next 24 hours.

WHY (rules fired):
  [F1] IF fainting THEN SELF_CARE - Most faints are brief and caused by a
     temporary drop in blood flow to the brain.
  [F10] IF fainting AND recovered_quickly THEN SELF_CARE - A quick recovery is
     reassuring.

RELATED GUIDES (TF-IDF / cosine ranking):
  - Fainting (Syncope)  (score 0.159)
      Fainting is a short loss of consciousness caused by a temporary
      reduction of blood flow to the brain. Common triggers are standing up
      quickly, heat...
  - Broken Bones and Sprains  (score 0.081)
      Do not give food or drink in case surgery is needed.

# RUN 5 - Typed question: 'I got a small cut on my finger'
##############################################################################
==============================================================================
[!] FOR AWARENESS ONLY - this is NOT a substitute for professional medical care.
    In any serious case CONTACT EMERGENCY SERVICES IMMEDIATELY (Egypt ambulance: 123 | International: 112).
==============================================================================
Understood facts : Cut or wound

DOES THIS CASE NEED A HOSPITAL?
  Probably manageable with first aid on the spot, but get medical help if it
  gets worse.

WHAT SHOULD I DO?
  1. Wash your hands (wear gloves if available).
  2. Apply firm, direct pressure with a clean cloth or gauze for 10 minutes;
     raise the injured limb above heart level.
  3. Once bleeding stops, rinse gently with clean running water and pat dry.
     Apply a sterile dressing.
  4. Check that the tetanus vaccination is up to date (booster if >5-10 years
     for dirty wounds).

WHY (rules fired):
  [C1] IF cut THEN SELF_CARE - A minor cut can be managed at home.

RELATED GUIDES (TF-IDF / cosine ranking):
  - Cuts, Wounds and Bleeding  (score 0.160)
      Most small cuts and scrapes can be treated at home. The main aims are to
      stop the bleeding, keep the wound clean and prevent infection.
  - Nosebleed  (score 0.062)
      Nosebleeds are common and are usually not serious. Most come from small
      blood vessels at the front of the nose.
